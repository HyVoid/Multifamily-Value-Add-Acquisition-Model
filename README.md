## Why I Built This Commercial Real Estate Underwriting Tool

Most commercial real estate pro forma models produce high IRR answers before validating whether the underlying property management assumptions are operationally achievable. 

In practice, value-add apartment acquisitions fail less often because of incorrect Excel formulas, and more often because of incorrect reasoning regarding market cycles and tenant absorption.

The most common analytical failures in real estate private equity include:
- Assuming 100% of units can immediately achieve market rent premiums, ignoring existing lease terms.
- Sizing acquisition debt based solely on Loan-to-Value (LTV) while ignoring lender Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) constraints.
- Treating interior renovation execution as instantaneous, rather than a phased monthly rollout.
- Valuing the exit sale price using trailing T12 NOI, which severely penalizes the sponsor's newly stabilized operations.
- Assuming operational improvements (like lowering expense ratios) occur without timeline friction.

I built this commercial real estate model after repeatedly observing situations where investment committees approved multifamily syndications based on internally inconsistent assumptions that were carefully hidden inside conventional, bloated underwriting spreadsheets.

Consider a simplified multi-family acquisition comparison:

The physical real estate asset did not change. Only the quality of the financial analysis framework changed.

This workbook functions as a productized reasoning system for real estate acquisitions. Its primary purpose is not merely to calculate Cash-on-Cash returns and Equity Multiples, but to expose the hidden operational assumptions that determine whether those returns are actually achievable.

### Three Traps That Catch Even Experienced Multifamily Investors

#### Trap 1: Market Rent Is Not Achievable Rent

A value-add acquisition was approved because market surveys suggested a 28% rent upside.

However, the existing lease structure delayed realization of that upside by nearly two years.

| Assumption               | Original Model | Corrected Model |
| ------------------------ | -------------: | --------------: |
| Units                    |            120 |             120 |
| Immediate Mark-to-Market |            Yes |              No |
| Average Lease Remaining  |        Ignored |       14 months |
| Levered IRR              |          20.8% |           13.9% |

The analytical error occurs because market rent and realizable rent are not equivalent variables.

The correct approach is to model:

* lease expiration schedules,
* loss-to-lease release timing,
* renovation downtime,
* post-renovation stabilization.

<details>
<summary>Formula logic</summary>

```excel
Loss_to_Lease =
Current_Market_Rent
-
Current_Actual_Rent

Released according to:
Lease_End_Month
```

</details>

---

#### Trap 2: LTV Does Not Determine Borrowing Capacity

A sponsor assumed financing availability at 75% LTV.

The lender ultimately approved financing based on Debt Yield constraints.

| Constraint | Maximum Loan |
| ---------- | -----------: |
| LTV        |       $15.0M |
| LTC        |       $14.2M |
| DSCR       |       $12.7M |
| Debt Yield |       $11.8M |

Final loan amount:

```text
$11.8M
```

The analytical failure occurs because lenders evaluate multiple risk constraints simultaneously.

The correct approach is to apply the minimum supported loan amount across all underwriting metrics. 

<details>
<summary>Formula logic</summary>

```excel
Final_Loan_Amount =
MIN(
LTV_Sized_Loan,
LTC_Sized_Loan,
DSCR_Sized_Loan,
Debt_Yield_Sized_Loan
)
```

</details>

---

#### Trap 3: Exit Values Depend On Future Income

An underwriting model calculated exit proceeds using Year-5 trailing NOI.

Institutional buyers priced the asset using forward stabilized income.

| Method       | Exit Value |
| ------------ | ---------: |
| Trailing NOI |     $33.1M |
| Forward NOI  |     $28.4M |

This difference reduced projected equity returns by over 400 basis points.

The analytical failure occurs because asset pricing reflects expected future income rather than historical income.

The corrected methodology capitalizes forward twelve-month NOI projections. 

<details>
<summary>Formula logic</summary>

```excel
Gross_Sale_Value =
Forward_12M_NOI
/
Exit_Cap_Rate
```

</details>

## The Business Logic & Methodology

At its core, this commercial real estate underwriting model shifts the analytical focus from *static assumptions* to *dynamic operational execution*. It solves complex multifamily acquisition problems by applying institutional-grade methodologies to everyday syndication math:

### 1. Dynamic Rent Roll & Lease Rollover (Solving the "Instant Income" Fallacy)
* **The Business Problem:** Traditional pro formas often assume rents jump to market rates on Day 1. This artificially inflates Year 1 Cash-on-Cash returns and hides early operating shortfalls.
* **The Methodology:** The model employs a **Phased Lease Rollover schedule**. It maps existing lease expirations and applies the target rent premiums (Loss-to-Lease capture) only as units naturally turn over. This produces a realistic Net Rental Income forecast based on actual tenant movement rather than theoretical averages.

### 2. CapEx Absorption & Unit Downtime (Solving the Equity Gap Risk)
* **The Business Problem:** General Partners (GPs) and syndicators frequently underestimate the working capital required to carry the property's fixed costs while units sit vacant during the value-add construction phase.
* **The Methodology:** A **Month-by-Month Renovation Pacing engine**. By defining the maximum units renovated per month and the average unit downtime, the model calculates the exact Capital Expenditures (CapEx) draw schedule and the resulting economic vacancy loss. This accurately sizes the total equity raise required to cover mid-project cash flow valleys.

### 3. Multi-Constraint Debt Sizing (Solving Lender Rejection)
* **The Business Problem:** Sizing an acquisition loan strictly on Loan-to-Value (LTV) ignores how agency lenders (Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac) or bridge debt funds actually underwrite risk.
* **The Methodology:** The template utilizes a **Four-Constraint Debt Engine**. It simultaneously calculates maximum allowable loan proceeds across LTV, Minimum DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio), Minimum Debt Yield, and Loan-to-Cost (LTC) hurdles. The model automatically restricts your leverage to the most conservative metric, ensuring your projected capital stack is actually fundable in the real world.

### 4. Forward-Looking Exit Valuation (Solving Reversion Mispricing)
* **The Business Problem:** Selling a newly repositioned apartment complex based on its Trailing 12 (T12) historical income severely discounts the sweat equity and value created by the sponsor's renovations.
* **The Methodology:** **Forward NOI Capitalization**. The model calculates the exit terminal value (disposition price) by applying the exit Cap Rate to the *forward* 12 months of stabilized Net Operating Income (NOI). This methodology accurately reflects the true market value of the repositioned asset, ensuring your LP Levered IRR and Equity Multiples are properly credited for the stabilization effort.
